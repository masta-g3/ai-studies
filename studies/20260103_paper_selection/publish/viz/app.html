I want to find the most interesting papers from a pool of ~200 recent AI publications. Rather than read all abstracts myself, I asked an LLM to select the top 5. This task—picking or ranking items from a collection—is increasingly common in LLM workflows, from content curation to research triage. But how reliable is a single response?


I tested two approaches: single-shot, where I take the model's first answer as-is, and consensus, where I run the same prompt 30 times and keep papers that appear in more than half the responses. The results below show what each method selected.

Results for :

Multi-run consensus clearly outperforms single-shot selection. The consensus papers—those chosen consistently across 30 runs—read as genuinely interesting when reviewing their abstracts: process reward models, representation hijacking, and test-time compute reuse. The single-shot selection, by contrast, includes two hallucinated arXiv codes that don't exist in the source pool. Only 1 of its 5 picks overlaps with the consensus set. For selection tasks, running multiple samples and taking majority votes is worth the extra compute.
